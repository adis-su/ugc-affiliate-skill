# Content Logic Matrix

This document defines how normalized user choices are translated into creative decisions.

## 1. Core distinction

- **Content Format** = how the content is executed.
- **Content Angle** = what the content communicates or sells.
- **Campaign Objective** = what the campaign is trying to achieve.
- **Campaign Stage** = where the viewer is in the decision journey.
- **CTA** = the desired next action.
- **Duration** = available time budget.
- **Scene Count** = production complexity constraint.
- **Platform** = distribution context and adaptation rules.

These inputs must not be treated as interchangeable.

---

## 2. Campaign Objective

| Objective | Primary creative priority | Default CTA tendency |
|---|---|---|
| Product Awareness | Recognition and memorable visual identity | None / Soft CTA |
| Product Discovery | Make the product immediately understandable and interesting | Soft CTA / View Product |
| Product Consideration | Demonstrate fit, use, styling, or specific benefits | View Product / Check Product |
| Affiliate Conversion | Reduce uncertainty and make the product visually desirable | Check Product / Shop Now |
| Product Launch | Introduce the product and establish its visual identity | Learn More / View Product |
| Product Education | Explain or demonstrate a specific product characteristic | Learn More / View Product |
| Brand / Product Introduction | Establish product and brand context | Soft CTA |

Objective controls emphasis. It does not automatically determine the format.

---

## 3. Campaign Stage

### Awareness
Prioritize:
- Immediate visual recognition
- Strong first-frame readability
- Low cognitive load
- Minimal explanation

### Consideration
Prioritize:
- Product details
- Fit and styling
- Demonstration
- Specific observations
- Trust-building behavior

### Conversion
Prioritize:
- Product visibility
- Clear benefit
- Reduced purchase uncertainty
- Stronger but still format-appropriate CTA

Stage should influence intensity, not force every video into a hard-sell structure.

---

## 4. CTA Rules

| CTA | Best use |
|---|---|
| None | Pure visual showcase, aesthetic, awareness |
| Soft CTA | Natural UGC and discovery |
| Check Product | Affiliate/product consideration |
| View Product | Product discovery and consideration |
| Shop Now | Conversion-oriented content |
| Learn More | Education or product introduction |
| Custom | User-defined intent |

Silent formats should normally express CTA visually or through on-screen text rather than spoken dialogue.

# 5. Content Format Matrix

## Talking Head

**Core behavior**
- Spoken dialogue required.
- Creator addresses camera directly.
- Facial expression and natural lip movement matter.
- Smartphone/social-native framing preferred.

**Best angles**
- Problem → Solution
- Product Review
- Product Showcase
- Product Education
- Try-On / First Impression
- Styling

**Avoid**
- Corporate presentation language.
- Over-scripted delivery.
- Excessive hand gestures.
- Commercial fashion-campaign staging.

---

## Silent Mirror Selfie

**Core behavior**
- No spoken dialogue by default.
- Behavioral script required.
- Mirror interaction is central.
- Outfit/product remains visually readable.
- Natural smartphone handling.

**Best angles**
- Outfit Inspiration
- Product Showcase
- Styling
- Everyday / Lifestyle
- Trend / Aesthetic
- Try-On / First Impression

**Default behavioral vocabulary**
- Look at outfit
- Adjust hem/sleeve
- Shift body weight
- Turn slightly
- Check phone screen
- Adjust hair
- Take one small step
- Return to relaxed stance

**Avoid**
- Runway poses.
- Repetitive posing.
- Perfectly symmetrical body positions.
- Excessive camera choreography.
- Studio/editorial aesthetics unless explicitly requested.

---

## Outfit Showcase

**Core behavior**
- Product silhouette and styling are the primary subject.
- Use front/side/detail views where useful.
- Minimal narrative.

**Best angles**
- Product Showcase
- Outfit Inspiration
- Styling
- Trend / Aesthetic

---

## Try-On

**Core behavior**
- Clear before/after or wearing sequence.
- Product interaction is important.
- Fit and silhouette must remain consistent.

**Best angles**
- Try-On / First Impression
- Product Showcase
- Problem → Solution
- Transformation
- Outfit Inspiration

---

## GRWM

**Core behavior**
- Sequential dressing/styling actions.
- Multiple product interactions.
- Can be silent, voice-over, or talking.

**Best angles**
- Styling
- Outfit Inspiration
- Everyday / Lifestyle
- Product Showcase

---

## Voice-over + B-roll

**Core behavior**
- Visual actions happen while narration carries information.
- B-roll should directly support spoken claims.

**Best angles**
- Product Review
- Product Education
- Problem → Solution
- Styling
- Product Showcase

---

## POV

**Core behavior**
- Camera represents the creator/viewer's perspective.
- Actions should have a clear situational reason.

**Best angles**
- Everyday / Lifestyle
- Problem → Solution
- Transformation
- Trend / Aesthetic
- Product Showcase

---

## Lifestyle

**Core behavior**
- Product appears naturally during an everyday activity.
- Avoid stopping the activity just to pose unless the concept calls for it.

**Best angles**
- Everyday / Lifestyle
- Outfit Inspiration
- Styling
- Product Showcase

---

## Product Showcase

**Core behavior**
- Product detail is dominant.
- Can use close-ups, handling, texture, fit, or movement.

**Best angles**
- Product Showcase
- Product Education
- Product Review

---

## Before / After

**Core behavior**
- Explicit visual contrast.
- Transformation must be easy to understand immediately.

**Best angles**
- Transformation
- Problem → Solution
- Try-On / First Impression
- Styling

---

## Unboxing

**Core behavior**
- Packaging/opening/product reveal sequence.
- Product should become progressively more visible.

**Best angles**
- Product Discovery
- Product Showcase
- Try-On / First Impression
- Product Review

---

## Hybrid

Use only when combining formats creates a meaningful creative advantage.

Examples:
- Talking Head + Mirror Selfie
- Voice-over + Try-On
- Talking Head + Product Detail
- GRWM + Silent Mirror

Do not use Hybrid merely to add complexity.

# 6. Content Angle Matrix

| Angle | Primary question answered |
|---|---|
| Product Showcase | What does the product actually look like? |
| Outfit Inspiration | What does a complete look using this product look like? |
| Styling | How can I wear/style this product? |
| Try-On / First Impression | How does it look or feel when worn? |
| Problem → Solution | What problem does this product help solve? |
| Product Review | What is the creator's observed experience? |
| Product Comparison | How does this differ from another option? |
| Transformation | What changes before vs after using/wearing it? |
| Everyday / Lifestyle | How does it fit into normal life? |
| Trend / Aesthetic | How can the product participate in a visual trend or aesthetic? |

---

# 7. Format × Angle Rules

The combination should produce a **creative treatment**, not simply concatenate two labels.

### Examples

**Silent Mirror Selfie + Outfit Inspiration**
- Show complete outfit.
- Use subtle pose and body-position changes.
- Emphasize silhouette and styling.
- No spoken script.

**Silent Mirror Selfie + Product Showcase**
- Prioritize product visibility.
- Include front/side/detail views.
- Keep movements minimal enough to preserve product readability.

**Silent Mirror Selfie + Styling**
- Show one or more styling adjustments.
- Make the change visually understandable.
- Use accessory, layering, tuck, sleeve, or footwear changes where relevant.

**Talking Head + Problem → Solution**
- Hook → problem → product introduction → concrete observation → CTA.
- Avoid generic claims without visible or product-supported evidence.

**Talking Head + Product Review**
- Personal context → specific observation → supporting visual → CTA.
- Favor conversational phrasing over advertising copy.

**Try-On + Transformation**
- Establish initial state → transition → final outfit → visual payoff.
- Keep product identity and creator identity consistent.

**GRWM + Styling**
- Sequential styling actions.
- Each action should contribute to the final look.
- Avoid filler actions that do not affect the outfit.

---

# 8. Duration Rules

Duration is a hard production budget.

| Duration | Default scene range | Creative behavior |
|---|---:|---|
| 4s | 1–2 | One idea, immediate visual payoff |
| 6s | 2–3 | Simple setup + payoff |
| 8s | 3–4 | Short progression |
| 10s | 4–5 | Complete micro-story or showcase |
| Custom | Auto | Calculate from format and action density |

If the requested scene count is too high for the duration, the skill should warn and recommend fewer scenes rather than compressing actions unrealistically.

---

# 9. Scene Count Rules

Default is **Auto**.

Auto calculation should consider:
- Duration
- Content format
- Content angle
- Number of required actions
- Dialogue density
- Product-detail requirements

Scene count is not the same thing as number of cuts. A scene should represent a meaningful visual state or action.

---

# 10. Platform Adaptation

The same creative concept may be distributed to multiple platforms, but platform-specific output can adjust:
- Hook density
- Text-safe areas
- CTA wording
- Caption/on-screen text recommendations
- Visual pacing
- Native social feel

Supported platforms:
- TikTok
- Instagram Reels
- Facebook Reels
- Shopee Video

Platform selection should not change the product facts or core creative concept unless required by the platform format.

---

# 11. Decision Pipeline

The normalized input should be processed in this order:

1. Validate product and retrieve/analyze product information.
2. Interpret campaign objective, stage, and CTA.
3. Build creator profile and identify realism constraints.
4. Resolve content format.
5. Resolve content angle.
6. Calculate duration budget.
7. Determine scene count.
8. Generate a creative concept from the combined constraints.
9. Generate dialogue or behavioral sequence according to format.
10. Build storyboard.
11. Generate image-reference prompts.
12. Generate frame-to-frame video prompts.
13. Run human-realism and product-consistency checks.
14. Adapt final output to selected platforms.

---

# 12. Non-Negotiable Creative Principle

The skill optimizes for **believable human UGC**, not generic cinematic perfection.

Default visual behavior should favor:
- Smartphone-native framing
- Natural body movement
- Small posture adjustments
- Realistic fabric behavior
- Imperfect but believable camera movement
- Ordinary environments where appropriate
- Product consistency
- Conversational language when speech is used

Avoid by default:
- Fashion-editorial posing
- Excessive cinematic camera movement
- Unrealistic body mechanics
- Perfectly symmetrical gestures
- Generic AI beauty styling
- Overly polished commercial staging
- Unnecessary visual effects
